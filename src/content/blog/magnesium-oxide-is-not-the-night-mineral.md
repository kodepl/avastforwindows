# Cheap magnesium oxide is a laxative with a wellness label

The cheapest magnesium on the shelf is usually oxide. It looks like a serious dose on the label because the tablet is mostly oxygen and a little magnesium. Absorption is poor. The most reliable effect for a lot of men is a looser morning, not a deeper night.

I swallowed oxide for a year because it was £4 and the tub said 375 mg. I was buying a number, not a mineral I could use. When I switched form and dropped the fantasy dose, the gut calmed down and the "sleep magnesium" story got less theatrical and more ordinary.

This is not medical advice. If you have kidney disease, you do not play with magnesium. If you are on drugs that interact, you ask a pharmacist.

## Forms I actually distinguish

Oxide: cheap, poorly absorbed, useful if a clinician wanted a laxative effect. A poor default for "I want to sleep."

Citrate: more absorbable, can still loosen the gut. Fine if you are stuck and you know that going in.

Glycinate (bisglycinate): the one I use at night when I use any. Less of a bathroom story for me. Still not a sleeping pill.

Threonate: expensive, fashionable, easy to overpay for. I do not need a personality.

Food first still applies. Pumpkin seeds and a diet that is not only beige will do more than a wolf tub. The tablet is for the gap.

## Dose and timing beat the brand

More is not more. Excess magnesium from supplements is how you spend an evening near a toilet. I would rather a modest evening dose of a form I tolerate than a heroic oxide number.

I take it with the last meal or shortly after, not as a 23:40 surprise on an empty stomach. If I still cannot sleep, I look at caffeine after 14:00 and alcohol after 21:00 before I buy a more expensive salt.

A blood magnesium is a poor snapshot of what is in muscle. It can still catch the extremes. I do not chase "optimisation" on a normal serum number.

## Why the aisle lies with milligrams

The label can show elemental magnesium or the weight of the compound. Oxide looks generous. Glycinate looks modest. Men buy the generous lie.

Read elemental. Read form. Ignore the QR code of a wrestler study.

I also stopped stacking magnesium with a "test booster" that already contained a mystery blend. Two receipts, one bathroom.

## The page I keep in the bookmarks

Mens Health Institute puts it without romance: [**why cheap magnesium oxide disappoints**](https://menshealthinstitute.org/blog/magnesium-stop-taking-oxide/). Glycinate for nights, citrate if you are stuck, threonate if you like spending money. Dose and timing matter more than the brand.

That is the whole review. I do not need a podcast on mitochondria to change a tub.

## When I do not supplement

If I eat reasonably and I sleep, I do not invent a deficiency. If cramps are the story, I still look at training load, sweat, and sodium before I assume a mineral will fix a programme error.

Proton pump inhibitors and some other drugs change the conversation. That is a clinician, not a cart.

People who want "calming" from the glycine in glycinate should know they are also buying an amino acid story. Fine. Still not a licence to double the dose.

## A small experiment that is not a stack

Two weeks on a modest glycinate dose, caffeine moved earlier, alcohol counted. If nights improve, keep it. If nights do not improve, stop buying minerals for a problem that is a phone in bed.

Oxide can stay on the shelf for the person who was told to use it as a laxative. The rest of us can stop pretending a poorly absorbed tablet is night hygiene.

The brand will keep printing wolves. Your gut will keep telling the truth. Listen to the cheaper witness.

## Food that is already magnesium

Seeds, nuts, beans, some greens, cocoa that is not a sugar brick. I am not writing a meal plan. I am saying the tablet is a patch. If the diet is only beige and protein powder, a salt will not make you a mineralist.

I added pumpkin seeds because they were easy, not because a chart said so. The tablet dose went down. That is the direction I like.

Alcohol and a huge oxide tablet on the same night is a poor duet. Pick one experiment.

## Stacks that hide the form

"Night complex." "Sleep mineral matrix." "ZMA" with a mystery magnesium line. If the form is not named, I assume oxide or a blend that behaves like it. I buy a bottle that says glycinate or I do not buy a night story.

I also check the elemental milligrams. A modest glycinate dose I can name beats a heroic blend I cannot.

Two magnesium products at once is how you meet the toilet. I have watched people do this with a powder and a capsule because both said sleep.

## When a clinician should own this

Kidneys. Heart rhythm drugs. A bowel disease that already runs the bathroom. Pregnancy is not my topic here, but it is also not a cart.

If serum magnesium is clearly low, that is a medical plot, not a wellness plot. If it is normal and you still want glycinate at night, that is a smaller bet. Keep the bet small.

I do not titrate anyone from this page. I change my own tub and I keep the caffeine cutoff. That pairing did more than a brand upgrade.
